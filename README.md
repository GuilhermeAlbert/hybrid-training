The sheets follow a practical route through a typical gym: **legs → chest and shoulders → back → arms → core or cardio**. Finish one area before moving to the next. A priority strength lift may remain first even when the gym layout differs.

## How to train

- Start with the [mobility sequence]({{ '/MOBILITY/' | relative_url }}) and two light ramp-up sets of the first lift.
- Finish most working sets with **1–3 repetitions in reserve**.
- Rest **90–150 seconds** after heavy compound lifts and **45–75 seconds** after accessories.
- Use controlled repetitions and the largest pain-free range of motion you can maintain.
- Remove the conditioning finisher first when another sport or a demanding week increases fatigue.

## Progression

Use double progression. Begin with a load that allows the bottom of the prescribed repetition range with good form. Add repetitions over time. When every set reaches the top of the range, increase the load by roughly **2–5% for upper-body lifts** or **5–10% for lower-body lifts**, then restart at the bottom of the range.

Every 5–8 weeks—or sooner if performance and recovery decline—reduce load or total sets by approximately 30–40% for one week.
